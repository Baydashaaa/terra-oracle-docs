Terra Oracle Classic

A Q&A, reputation and draw protocol on Terra Classic, paid for in LUNC.

Ask the community a question and pay for it in LUNC. Answer someone else's and earn reputation. Every fee, every draw and every point of reputation is settled on Terra Classic, where you can check it without asking us to confirm anything.

You post under your wallet address, not under a name. The address is stored with the question, with every answer and with every vote, and it stays publicly readable - that is what lets anyone verify a question was paid for and that a vote was cast once. So this is pseudonymity, not anonymity: nothing here ties the address to your identity, but everything you do from one address can be linked together, on this site and on the chain.

What you can do

Oracle Q&A

DAO Chat

Oracle Draw
Daily and Weekly draws. The winner is computed from a secret committed before entries close, published afterwards so anyone can recheck it.

Seven ranks, earned by contributing. Held in a smart contract, not our database.

Show up regularly for a REP multiplier, cheaper questions and free entries.
Treasury
Where the fees go, split on a fixed schedule to public wallets.

What is Oracle Q&A?

Oracle Q&A is an on-chain question and answer system. Anyone can submit a question by paying a fee in LUNC. Every fee is split 50/50 between the Weekly Draw Pool and the Protocol Treasury, and the pool half is what earns the author free Weekly Draw entries.

What is DAO Chat?

DAO Chat is a permanent on-chain messaging system. Every message is stored on Terra Classic blockchain and is publicly visible forever. The cost per message is 5,000 LUNC, which goes entirely to the Protocol Treasury.

Free entries are tracked automatically via on-chain transaction history, updated every hour into a file committed to a public repository, and sent into the pool contract before the round closes. From there they weigh the odds exactly like a minted ticket.

The contract refuses them once the round has closed. That refusal is the point: after the close the operator can already tell where the result points, so a ticket added then would be a ticket aimed. What you can check yourself is the file's commit history against what the contract holds - both are public, and they should say the same thing.

These entries carry no money into the pot, and they do not need to: the fees for the messages and questions that earn them already go to the same pool. They are free to the participant, not to the protocol.

How winners are selected

Winners are selected by a commit-reveal scheme in the pool contract. A round is meant to be opened - and its secret committed as a hash - before it takes a single entry. After entries close the secret itself is published, and the contract derives the winner from the secret, the entries and the round number. Once the hash is on the chain the secret behind it is fixed, so it cannot be swapped afterwards to pick a different winner, and anyone can recompute the result once it is revealed.

One honest exception. The contract allows a round to be opened late rather than refusing entries with no round to hold them - refusing would let the pool deadlock. When that happens the round is flagged has_late_entries, and it means the commitment came after at least one entry already existed. For those entries the pre-commit guarantee above does not hold, because their entropy was already visible when the secret was chosen. Weekly round 4 is settled with this flag set. Check the flag on any round you care about; every round carries it, and the answer is public.

commitment = sha256(secret)  ·  published before entries open
winner = f(secret, entries, round_id)  ·  recomputable after reveal

Entries weight the odds, they do not guarantee a win. A Legendary holder puts 10 tickets in the pool and a Common holder puts 1 - but a single ticket can still be the one drawn. More entries mean better odds, never certainty.

What the commitment guarantees. Once the hash is published, the secret behind it is fixed: the result cannot be steered afterwards, and re-running the reveal always gives the same winner. And a round no longer depends on us publishing the secret at all - if it is not revealed in time, anyone can settle the round from data that was already fixed when entries closed, and the prize is paid. So the schedule is ours to keep, but the round happening is not ours to decide. What we do still know is the outcome, as soon as entries close; moving to a source of randomness that nobody derives is on our list.

Daily Draw - step by step

  1. All Oracle Mask mints registered to the Daily pool since the last draw are collected from the contract’s on-chain mint events
  2. Ticket array is built: Common = 1 ticket, Rare = 5, Legendary = 10
  3. After the 20:00 UTC deadline the round is closed and its secret is revealed on-chain, matching the hash committed before the round opened
  4. The contract derives the winner from the revealed secret, the round's entry entropy and the round number
  5. Winner receives 80% of the prize pool automatically on-chain
  6. 10% → Protocol Treasury · 10% stays as seeds for next round
  7. If fewer than 5 entries - draw is skipped, all funds carry to next round

Weekly Draw - step by step

  1. All Oracle Mask mints registered to the Weekly pool that have not yet been used in a completed draw are collected - if a draw is skipped, those entries carry over instead of expiring
  2. Free entries from Oracle Q&A, DAO Chat and streaks are sent into the contract before the round closes, and join the same ticket list
  3. Up to three winners are selected from the same deadline-block hash. Each place uses a seed derived from the previous one, so the chain has to be replayed in order - the seed for 2nd place is not built from the block hash directly:
    seed₀ = block_hash
    1st: seed₁ = sha256(seed₀ + "0")  →  index = BigInt("0x" + seed₁) % total  →  48%
    2nd: seed₂ = sha256(seed₁ + "1")  →  index = BigInt("0x" + seed₂) % total  →  20%
    3rd: seed₃ = sha256(seed₂ + "2")  →  index = BigInt("0x" + seed₃) % total  →  12%
  4. Places are kept distinct by minter, not by current owner. This is a separate rule, not a property of the seed chain: if the drawn index belongs to a minter who already took a place, the index moves forward one ticket at a time until it reaches a new one, wrapping around to index 0 at the end of the list. The minter is the address recorded when the NFT was minted, which is cheap for the contract to read - the current owner would cost a query per candidate. Two consequences worth stating plainly: one person holding NFTs minted by different addresses can take more than one place, and two NFTs from the same minter stay mutually exclusive even after being transferred to different people
  5. The prize follows the token, not the ticket. Each place is paid to whoever owns the winning NFT at the moment the round is settled. A transfer after entries close but before settlement therefore changes who receives the prize
  6. Fewer than three participants means fewer places. With two unique wallets only 1st and 2nd are paid; with one, only 1st. The share of an unfilled place is not paid out - it stays in the pool wallet and becomes part of next week's pool
  7. 10% → Protocol Treasury · 10% stays as seeds for next round
  8. If fewer than 5 entries - draw is skipped, all funds carry to next week
  9. If the pool balance is below 500,000 LUNC - draw is skipped as well, and the pool carries to next week

Verify it yourself

Every draw records what is needed to reproduce the result in winners.json. Two schemes have been used, so start by reading the round's randomness field.

Rounds settled by the pool contract

randomness: "oracle-pool-commit-reveal". These rounds record seed_hash, secret, entropy and result. The first check is the important one and needs nothing but a hash function:

  1. sha256(secret) must equal seed_hash. The hash was published on-chain before the round opened, so this proves the revealed secret is the one that was committed and could not have been chosen after seeing the entries
  2. result = sha256(secret || entropy || round_id), with round_id as 8 bytes big-endian. It must equal the recorded result
  3. Each place walks the seed chain: seed₀ = sha256(result || 0), then seedₙ = sha256(seedₘ || n), place number again as 8 bytes big-endian. The index is the first 16 bytes of the seed read as a big-endian integer, modulo the ticket count
  4. If that index belongs to a minter who already took a place, it moves forward one ticket at a time, wrapping at the end, until it reaches a new one. Uniqueness is by recorded minter, not by current owner, and the prize goes to whoever owns the winning NFT at settlement
sha256(secret) == seed_hash
result = sha256(secret · entropy · round_idbe8)
seedₙ = sha256(seedₘ · nbe8)  →  index = u128(seedₙ[0..16]) % total_entries

entropy is folded over the round's entries in order and is recorded with the round, so you can take it as given for this check - or rebuild it from the contract's entry list if you want the whole chain.

Rounds settled without a reveal

randomness: "oracle-pool-stale-fallback", and secret is empty. The secret was never published, so after a waiting period anyone was able to settle the round from what was already fixed when it closed:

result = sha256("oracle-pool:stale" · entropy · round_idbe8 · seed_hash)

From there the places are picked exactly as above. Every input was public before the settlement, so this outcome is the same no matter when it is triggered - waiting for a better moment gains nothing. A round marked this way means the reveal did not arrive on time, not that anything about the entries changed.

Rounds drawn before the move to the contract

Any other value of randomness, with ticket_rule chain-v1 or chain-v1+free. These used a Terra Classic block hash:

  1. Take block_height and fetch that block from any Terra Classic LCD (its block_id.hash, base64 → hex uppercase, must equal the recorded block_hash)
  2. Rebuild the ticket list, or use the frozen snapshot in rounds/<round_id>.json - the exact ticket order used, as pairs of [wallet, ticket count]
  3. For a Daily round: compute BigInt("0x" + block_hash) % total_entries - it must equal the recorded winner_index
  4. For a Weekly round: replay the seed chain above in order, applying the duplicate-skip rule after each place
Both schemes fix the result before it is known to anyone reading the page, and both can be recomputed from public data. Older rounds stay checkable by the rule they were drawn under.

Protocol

Circuit

A 250-zone board. Claim zones, a marker runs across it, and whoever owns the zone it stops on takes the round.

Circuit is not a lottery - like the draws, it exists to stimulate on-chain economic activity.

Where to play

Circuit runs on draw.terraoracle.io, on the same page as the Daily and Weekly draws. You need an Oracle Mask to take part - the tier you hold sets how many zones you may claim in a single round.

How a round works

Zones are handed out back to back from the first free one. You choose how many to take, not which ones - so the board fills in order and everything past the last claimed zone sits out the round.

A round draws when the board fills, or every three hours once at least 20 zones are taken. Below that minimum nothing is lost and nothing is refunded: the zones and the pot carry into the next round, which is also why several quiet night rounds merge into one busier morning round.

Zone price

The first zone costs 1,000 LUNC and each further zone costs 5% more. The markup is cumulative per wallet per round, so splitting one purchase into several transactions does not avoid it.

TCO rewards

Twelve percent of every round becomes TCO: half is distributed to everyone who held zones that round, and half goes to the burn side. That half is transferred to terra10zptfez4jdvakrhu58q4nqj2te7mnpewqhu27a and held there - the irreversible burn is deferred until the token's whitelist allows it. Holding tokens at an address is not burning them, and the balance can be checked like any other.

The winner's share is the prize bucket, not always the whole of it: by default 5% of that bucket is split between the owners of zones adjacent to the winning one when those zones belong to somebody else. With no eligible neighbour the winner takes it all. This split is an operator setting rather than a contract rule, and every round's snapshot records what was actually paid.

Your share is fixed in LUNC the moment a round draws, in proportion to the zones you held - and converted to TCO in a single batch once 50,000 LUNC has built up. Buying a few cents' worth of TCO after every round would cost more in fees than it is worth, so the purchase waits for the threshold. Until then your share is recorded and safe, and the rewards panel will show nothing to claim.

Claims are cumulative: the contract stores what you have already taken and pays the difference, so nothing expires and many epochs can be claimed in one transaction. See Contracts for the reward contract and how to verify a claim.

Verify the result

The winning zone comes from the Terra Classic block hash at the round's deadline - not from a number chosen by a server. The block is taken by deadline rather than by run time, so restarting the closer cannot change the outcome, and there is no fallback to a clock-based value: if the chain is unreachable the round simply stays open.

Every closed round records the block it used, so anyone can fetch that block themselves and repeat the calculation.

Oracle Reputation

Earn REP through activity. Unlock ranks, fee discounts, and weekly rewards.

What is Oracle Reputation?

Oracle Reputation (REP) measures what you have contributed to the protocol - asking, answering, being upvoted, taking part. It is not a token: it cannot be bought, sent or sold, and it belongs to the wallet that earned it. What it does is unlock ranks, fee discounts and a share of the weekly reward pool.

REP is held in a smart contract on Terra Classic, not in our database. Your profile reads the figure from a public node, so the number you see is the number the chain holds - and you can check it without asking us. How that works, and what the contract does and does not enforce, is under Oracle Score.

Two figures, one score. Your rank is based on all-time REP and never decays - take a month off and your status is where you left it. Your weight, which counts for draw entries and voting, decays with a 90-day half-life, so influence over what happens now reflects what you are contributing now. Both are shown on your profile.

Anti-abuse limits: Answers are limited to 3 per question per day per wallet. Voting is capped at 20 votes per day per wallet. Self-votes are blocked. Answer authors can delete their own answers.

Degressive answer REP. Answering is the only free way to earn REP, so the rate decreases within a day: answers 1–3 earn +40 each, answers 4–10 earn +10 each, and later answers earn 0. You are never blocked from answering — only the REP stops accruing, and the counter resets daily.

Upvote source check. An upvote grants REP to the answer author only when it comes from a wallet with paid on-chain history — a chat message, an NFT mint, or a question. Creating a wallet costs only gas, so ungated upvotes would be REP with no spend behind them. Anyone can still vote; the vote count on the answer always goes up.

Daily Streak

The Daily Streak system rewards consistent protocol activity. Every day you interact with Terra Oracle Classic through a paid action, your streak grows - unlocking REP multipliers, fee discounts, and exclusive Draw bonuses.

What counts as a streak day

Your streak advances by 1 each day you complete at least one of the following paid actions:

ActionCost
Ask a question (Oracle Q&A)200,000 LUNC (or discounted price)
Send a message (DAO Chat)5,000 LUNC
Enter Daily DrawNFT mint fee
Enter Weekly DrawNFT mint fee

Free actions such as answering questions, voting, or poll voting do not count toward your streak.

Grace period

Missing one day every 7 days does not break your streak. The system automatically applies a grace period - the missed day is skipped and your streak continues as normal. However, only one grace is allowed per 7-day rolling window. Missing two or more days in a row always resets the streak to 1.

Streak milestones & rewards

Reaching a milestone unlocks permanent bonuses that apply as long as your streak is active. The REP multiplier is applied to all reputation points you earn.

REP multiplier

The streak multiplier applies to your total base REP score. For example, at a 14-day streak (×1.5), if your base REP is 1,000 your displayed reputation becomes 1,500. The multiplier is only active while your streak is running - if your streak resets, your REP returns to the base value.

Question discount

At 7+ days streak you get 25% off any question tariff - Basic drops from 50,000 to 37,500 LUNC, Priority from 200,000 to 150,000 LUNC. The discount comes out of the Treasury half only, so your free Weekly Draw entries are unaffected. It does not stack with your rank discount - the system always applies whichever discount is higher. For example, an ARCHON rank user (20% off) with a 7-day streak receives 25% off, not 45%.

14-day free entries

When you first reach a 14-day streak, 50,000 LUNC is automatically transferred from the Protocol Reserve wallet to the Weekly Draw pool. This corresponds to 2 free entries (25,000 LUNC each - the price of a Common NFT). The transfer is processed by an automated script that runs every hour.

Trusted User status (30 days)

Reaching 30 consecutive days unlocks Trusted User status. This is a permanent achievement - even if your streak resets, the status and golden profile border remain. As a Trusted User, every Draw entry you make receives an automatic +1 bonus entry at no extra cost, as long as your current streak is 30 or more days.

Streak in Your Stats

If you miss more than one day (outside the grace period), your streak resets to 1 on your next active day. Milestones already reached are never removed - one-time rewards such as the 14-day LUNC transfer and the Trusted User golden border are permanent. What the chain settles

NFT mints. Payment, token creation and the pool transfer are one atomic transaction. A wallet cannot be charged without receiving the NFT.
Reputation. Balances, the degressive answer ladder, daily limits and decay all live in the Oracle Score contract. Nobody can edit a score in a database.
Every payment. Questions, chat messages, mints, draw prizes and treasury distributions are transfers you can look up in an explorer.

What is still off-chain

Being straight about this matters more than sounding decentralised.

Questions & answersStored in questions.json, committed to a public Git repository. Every edit is a commit with a timestamp, so the history is auditable even though the store is not a chain.
Free actionsAnswers and upvotes cannot be paid for on-chain by the author, so a Cloudflare Worker verifies them and an attestor key writes them into the contract on a schedule. That key can only touch actions the contract has explicitly opted in, and every grant is a public transaction.
StreaksDay counting and grace days stay in storage. A streak no longer changes your rank or the REP figure you see — both come from the contract alone, so they can be checked against it. The multiplier still applies to your weekly reward share, and a seven-day streak still carries its own question discount.
Draw executionThe winner is derived from a block hash on Terra Classic, not from a random number the operator controls, and the payout is an ordinary transfer. The list of participants is rebuilt from the NFT contract by a published rule, so it no longer depends on us reporting it correctly. What is still off-chain is the payout itself, signed by a scheduled script, and — for the Weekly draw only — the free entries earned by chatting and asking. See Oracle Draw for how to check a past draw yourself.
Payment routingThe frontend splits a question payment between the Weekly pool and the Treasury. The contract can enforce this split itself, and that is a planned step — today the amounts are simply fixed and public.
One admin key. Contract parameters and migrations are controlled by a single key held by the developer. It is not a multisig, and calling it one would be dishonest at the current size of the community. Moving to a multisig is planned as the protocol grows.

How the draw winner is chosen

Not by the script that runs the draw. The winner is derived by the pool contract from a secret whose hash was committed before the round opened, so re-running the reveal cannot change the outcome - the full rule, what the operator can and cannot do, and how to recheck a past draw yourself, are under Oracle Draw.

What still requires trust. The ticket list is reproducible: every entry is an NFT held by the contract, and the rule that turns those into tickets is published — mint time decides order, tier decides weight. The randomness is checkable after the fact: the hash of each round's secret goes on-chain before the round opens, so the result is fixed from that moment and anyone can recompute it once the secret is published. Publishing it on time is our part of the deal; a round that goes unrevealed carries over with its entries and prize rather than being lost. Weekly free entries are earned off-chain, so the count comes from us - what backs it is the hourly commit history of the file they are counted in, and the fact the contract stops accepting them the moment the round closes.

What the contract guarantees

Atomic mint. Payment, NFT creation and the transfer into the Daily or Weekly pool are one transaction. If any step fails the whole transaction reverts — a wallet can never be charged without receiving the NFT.
No free mints. The CW721 minter is the contract itself, so the standard mint entry point is rejected for every external caller, including the admin. The only way to obtain an Oracle Mask is to pay the tier price.
Price is read from the chain. The interface queries the live tier price before signing, and the contract rejects any amount that does not match exactly (Wrong payment). Under- and overpayment are both impossible.
Burn tax. Terra Classic applies a 0.5% burn tax to the contract’s outgoing transfer, so a pool receives 99.5% of the mint price. The contract itself forwards 100% and keeps no balance.

Mint message

A mint is a single MsgExecuteContract carrying the exact tier price as funds.

entropy is 32 random bytes generated in your browser. The pool contract folds it into the round, which is what stops whoever holds the round's secret from minting until the result points at them — they cannot know what later minters will add. It is required for any pool that records entries on-chain, and ignored for one that does not.

Two numbers, one score

A single figure cannot serve both purposes, so the contract keeps two and returns both from one query.

lifetime_earned
Rank		Never decays. Drives your rank, fee discount and reward multiplier. Taking a break does not cost you the status you earned — all-time REP never resets.
effective
Weight		Decays with a 90-day half-life, so influence has to be maintained: a single burst of activity does not buy permanent standing. It is published for transparency and shown on your profile, but nothing currently reads it - draw entries come from NFTs recorded in the pool contract, and governance counts one vote per wallet. Weighting them by effective is planned, not active.
A third figure, the epoch score, buckets each 7-day week and never decays. It decides weekly reward shares, where the fair basis is what you contributed that week.

How much each action grants

The amounts and the answer ladder are documented under Reputation; the contract holds the same figures as configuration. Two of its rules are worth stating here because they are enforced rather than described:

Ladder scopeCounted per wallet per UTC day, across all questions. Answering a busy thread late never costs you the full rate - the limit is on how much one person earns in a day, not on how popular a question is.
Variable grantsMost actions are worth a fixed amount set in the config. An NFT mint is worth 25, 125 or 250 by tier, so the draw action is marked variable_amount and the figure is supplied with the record. Every other action rejects a supplied amount outright, which is what stops the recording key from deciding its own rewards.

How an action reaches the chain

Paid actions are verified against the chain before anything is recorded; free ones cannot be, so they take a different route.

1 · ActYou ask, answer, upvote or send a message.
2 · VerifyFor paid actions the payment transaction is checked on-chain: sender, recipient and amount. The hash is registered so it can never be reused.
3 · QueueThe grant is queued. Queueing started before reputation moved on-chain, so nothing was lost in the transition.
4 · RecordAn hourly job submits the queued grants to the contract as a batch. Records are cleared only after the transaction is confirmed in a block, so a failed run retries rather than dropping grants.
The attestor key is deliberately weak. It can record actions and nothing else — it cannot change parameters, migrate the contract or slash anyone, and every grant it makes is capped and publicly visible. It holds gas and nothing more.

Help

FAQ

Short answers to the questions that come up most often. If something is missing, ask in DAO Chat.

Minting

Where do I mint Oracle Mask NFTs?
Only on draw.terraoracle.io, directly from the Oracle Mask contract. There is no marketplace step and no address to send LUNC to manually - never send LUNC to a wallet address to mint.
What happens if my mint transaction fails?
Nothing is charged. Payment, NFT creation and the transfer into the prize pool are a single transaction: if any part fails, the whole thing reverts and the LUNC stays in your wallet. It is not possible to pay and not receive the NFT.
I minted but the NFT is not in My Bag yet
Give it a few seconds and reload - the page reads directly from the blockchain, so the NFT appears as soon as the block is confirmed. Nothing has to catch up afterwards: the mint, the payment and the entry record in the pool contract are one transaction, so if the entry had failed you would have no NFT and no charge. Your NFT is in your wallet on-chain, and the pool holds a separate entry record pointing at its token id.
Is there a limit on how many I can mint?
No. You can mint any number of NFTs, in any tier, in the same round. Every NFT adds its own entries to the pool you selected at mint time.
Why did the prize pool receive slightly less than I paid?
Terra Classic deducts a burn tax from the outgoing transfer. The rate is chain configuration rather than a protocol constant - on the protocol's payouts of 31 August and 7 September 2026 it came to 1.5%, so the pool received 98.5% of what was sent. The contract forwards 100% of your payment and keeps nothing for itself; the chain takes its cut in transit.

Draws

Do I need to do anything after minting to enter the draw?
No. The pool you choose at mint time - Daily or Weekly - is written into the NFT itself, and the entry is registered automatically. You will see the NFT marked as active for that draw in My Bag.
Is my NFT burned when I enter a draw?
No. The NFT stays in your wallet permanently. After the draw runs it is marked as having played, so it no longer counts toward new rounds, but you keep the token - it remains transferable and tradable.
What happens if fewer than 5 entries are in a round?
The draw is skipped and the entire pool carries over to the next round. Nothing is lost and no partial payout happens.
How do I know the draw is not rigged?
Each round's secret is committed as a hash before the round opens, and revealed after entries close; the pool contract derives the winner from the revealed secret, the entries and the round number. Because the commitment is fixed first, the result cannot be steered afterwards, and anyone can recompute it - the full formula is in the Oracle Draw section. What this does not give you is unpredictability: we derive the secret, so we know the outcome from the moment entries close. If the reveal never comes, anyone can settle the round by a fallback rule instead, so withholding it does not cancel the draw. Every entry and every payout is an on-chain transaction and can be replayed independently on Terra Finder.
Can I win more than once in the same weekly draw?
No. The three weekly places go to three distinct minters - the address recorded when each NFT was minted. Two NFTs minted by the same address cannot both win, even after being transferred apart. The flip side: if you hold NFTs that were minted by different addresses, they are treated as different entrants and can take more than one place. Each prize is paid to whoever owns the winning NFT when the round settles.

NFTs & the contract

Can I transfer or sell my Oracle Mask?
Yes. The collection follows the standard CW721 interface, so transfers, approvals and marketplace listings all work the same way as with any other Terra Classic NFT.
What do the token numbers mean?
Token IDs are sequential per tier - common-1, rare-7, legendary-2 - and are displayed as Common #1, Rare #7 and so on. The number is assigned by the contract and can never repeat.
Can the contract be changed after deployment?
The contract keeps a migration admin so new mechanics can be added later - NFT crafting is next on the roadmap. A migration preserves the contract address, all existing NFTs and their metadata. Every upgrade is documented here and stays verifiable through the published code checksum.
Can anyone mint an NFT for free?
No. The contract itself is the CW721 minter, so the standard mint entry point is rejected for every external caller - including the protocol admin. Paying the exact tier price is the only path to an Oracle Mask.

Reputation & general

Does REP cost anything? Can I buy it?
REP is not a token and cannot be bought, sold or transferred. It is earned purely through activity - asking and answering questions, chat messages, upvotes received, and NFT mints - and it determines your rank, fee discounts and weekly reward share.
Where is my REP actually stored?
In a smart contract on Terra Classic, not in our database. Your profile reads the figure straight from a public node and shows a verify link so you can look up the same number in an explorer. See Oracle Score.
Why did my REP not go up immediately after answering?
Answers and upvotes are free, so you cannot pay for them on-chain yourself - a scheduled job records them instead, and it runs about once an hour. Your profile shows how many actions are still queued. Nothing is lost while you wait; the grant is recorded whether or not you are looking.
My rank stayed but my draw weight dropped. Why?
They are two different figures on purpose. Rank uses all-time REP and never decays, so your status and fee discount survive a break. Weight decays with a 90-day half-life, because influence over what happens now should reflect what you are contributing now. Both are shown on your profile.
Can you change my REP?
Not by rewriting it. Bulk seeding was used once to carry over reputation earned before the contract existed, and it has since been closed permanently - the contract will refuse it from any caller, including the admin key. What remains is a Slash for abuse, which is a public transaction with its reason recorded on the chain.
Which wallets are supported?
Keplr, Galaxy Station, Terra Station, Luncdash and other Terra Classic compatible wallets. You need LUNC in the wallet for both the action itself and the network gas fee.
Where can I verify everything myself?
Every wallet, mint and payout is public. Use Terra Finder for transactions, the Smart Contract section for direct chain queries and the reproducible build steps, and the Treasury section for the full wallet list.
